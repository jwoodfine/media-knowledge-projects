The **Croatia regional market index** lists the nine Croatian markets that meet the co-location system's composition gates. The nine markets run
the length of the country, from Zagreb in the north to Split, Zadar, and Šibenik along the
Dalmatian coast, rather than clustering around a single metropolitan core.

## Coverage

No rank or score is published for any market in this table. Every market below qualifies under
the framework's Regional Market composition gate — a hypermarket anchor paired with at least two
of {hardware, price club, lifestyle, electronics, sport} across the market's co-location clusters.
Full methodology: [[about-regional-markets-system|Regional Markets Intelligence System]].

The metropolitan reference point recorded for each market is the nearest major metropolitan
centre in the framework's continental reference set — a straight-line (crow-flies) distance
measurement, not a drive-time or commuting estimate. It is not a claim that the market functions
as a commuter suburb of that centre. Every one of Croatia's nine reference points sits outside the
country: Graz, Austria (three entries), Timișoara, Romania (two entries), Trieste, Italy (one
entry), and Ancona, Italy (three entries). This is a consequence of the framework's continental
reference-point set, not a claim about any real commuting relationship, and three of the nine
reference points cross the Adriatic Sea rather than a land border. Zagreb, Croatia's own capital
and largest city, is itself among the nine. It carries Regional Market classification and a Graz
reference point despite functioning as the country's primary metropolitan core, not a suburb of
any other city.

Catchment population has not been computed for any of the nine Croatian markets in the current
dataset build — Croatia is one of six European countries without this figure in the 2026-08-07
release, alongside Bulgaria, Finland, Norway, Romania, and Sweden. Each profile page states this
plainly rather than presenting an unavailable figure as zero or omitting the gap silently.

## Retail and Civic Anchors

Kaufland and Pevex anchor all nine clusters — the only two chains present in every entry, a
hypermarket and hardware pairing rather than the single dominant chain seen in Greece. Interspar
(hypermarket) and Hervis (sport) each appear in seven of the nine; Decathlon (sport) in six;
Bauhaus (hardware) in five. Civic infrastructure varies sharply by market: Zagreb and Split each
draw on their namesake public universities' dozens of faculties and multiple major hospitals,
while Zadar, Varaždin, and Pula anchor around a single named regional university. Sisak and
Slavonski Brod each carry a single satellite University of Zagreb faculty rather than a full
campus. Šibenik's civic anchor is a public open-learning institution rather than a
degree-granting university, and Osijek's two clusters record no civic anchor at all in the current
dataset — a real, disclosed gap rather than an editorial omission.

## AEC Data Coverage

Climate, seismic, and wildfire-hazard data has been computed for none of Croatia's nine clusters
to date — a wider gap than Greece's two of seven. Coverage for all nine clusters — Zagreb, Osijek,
Pula, Sisak, Slavonski Brod, Split, Varaždin, Zadar, and Šibenik — is pending in the current
dataset build; see [[gis-data-overview|GIS Data Overview]] for the AEC data pipeline.
