Every Woodfine Building is planned from the furniture out, not from a floor-plate area formula in. A leasehold arrives with its light, circulation, and ventilation already proven at three nested scales — the Key Plan, the Tile, and the Floor Plate — so no building is sized larger than the accommodation it was designed to hold.

## The Key Plan

The Key Plan is the smallest unit of space worth leasing: a room-scale plan sized from real furniture placement, real circulation, and real daylight access, rather than from a generic area-per-person allowance. Most Key Plans resolve into three zones moving in from the building face — a daylight zone where desks or workstations sit within reach of natural light, a flexible interior zone used for storage or depth that varies by tenant category, and a circulation zone. Private Office Key Plans are the one documented exception: they carry no separate circulation zone of their own, opening directly onto the building's shared corridor instead. Key Plan sizes vary by professional tenant category — private office, laboratory, medical, academic, business, and civic space each have their own range, from single-office suites up to large civic assemblies.

## The Tile

Key Plans combine into Tiles. A Tile is a block of Key Plans that does double duty: it is the unit a tenant actually leases, and it is the single climate-services zone the building's mechanical systems are sized to — every Key Plan sharing a Tile shares that Tile's own heating, cooling, and ventilation zone. Not every Key Plan fits every Tile; at building corners and around elevator lobbies, smaller purpose-built configurations fill space that a standard Tile would not fit efficiently. Two development classes — Retail Select and Tech Industrial — skip the Tile level entirely: Key Plans in those classes compose directly into Floor Plates.

## The Floor Plate

The Floor Plate is the [[fixed-floor-plates|fixed floor plate]] a Woodfine Building's structural grid is built around: Tiles plus the building's core — the shared elevator lobbies, stairs, mechanical rooms, and washrooms every Floor Plate carries regardless of tenant mix.

## Nesting Without Remainder

Key Plans nest into Tiles, and Tiles nest into Floor Plates, without leaving unaccounted space at any level — but not by simple addition. Where a clean sum would leave a gap or an overhang, smaller purpose-built configurations absorb the difference, the same way Special Tiles absorb the odd geometry at a building corner. A prospective tenant is selecting from a defined set of geometrically complete configurations at every scale, not requesting a custom partition arrangement after the fact.

## Self-Similar, Not Repeating

The same nesting logic repeats at every scale of the hierarchy — a Key Plan nests into a Tile the same way a Tile nests into a Floor Plate — which is what makes the system self-similar. It is not, however, periodic: the sequence of Key Plans and Tiles along any one floor is set by the actual tenant mix on that floor, not by a repeating grid. Because Key Plan widths vary by tenant category rather than following one fixed module, a single Floor Plate can offer meaningfully more distinct leasehold size combinations than a floor built from one uniform unit repeated to fill the space — real optionality for a prospective tenant, not a marketing description of flexibility.
